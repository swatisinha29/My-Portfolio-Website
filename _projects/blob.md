> **In one sentence:** commerce worked, a lead-based services marketplace did not, and a delivery-partner pilot showed that logistics is what decides whether a marketplace can scale.

## The problem

During COVID, customers struggled to get essentials, while local sellers lost their main source of revenue. Existing platforms were unreliable in smaller cities because of delivery constraints.

## Vision

Build a hyperlocal commerce ecosystem connecting **local sellers**, **local service providers** and **nearby customers**.

**North Star metric:** successful orders delivered per day.

## Users

| User | Context | Need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers | Households in lockdown | Essential goods and services |
| Sellers | Small local shops with no digital presence | An immediate revenue channel |

## Product strategy

1. **MVP:** a 0→1 launch.
2. **Scale:** multi-city expansion.
3. **Services:** a marketplace for local service providers.
4. **Logistics experimentation:** a delivery-partner pilot.

## What I owned

I owned the full product lifecycle: market research, user and vendor journey mapping, competitive analysis and A/B testing. That work lifted conversion from **38% to 63%**, cut cancellations to **under 10%** and brought fulfillment reliability to **about 80%**. I also built and maintained the backend in Node.js and MongoDB, including the APIs and workflow systems behind marketplace operations.

Key features covered commerce, the order flow, and services, which ran on a lead-based model.

## Challenges

### 1. Logistics was the core bottleneck

In cities without delivery partners, fulfillment was inconsistent.

### 2. The services marketplace failed

It worked as a lead marketplace, but failed as a fulfillment product. Why it broke:

- There was no standard pricing or scheduling system.
- It depended heavily on offline coordination.
- There was no visibility into the quality of completed services.

## What I learned

- Lead generation is not a full marketplace.
- Offline coordination breaks the user experience.
- Without pricing and scheduling, users lose trust.
- Services need tighter control than commerce.

## If I rebuilt it today

- **Logistics first.**
- **Replicate the delivery-partner pilot's success.**
- **A managed marketplace for services**, with pricing, scheduling and quality under the platform's control.

## Final impact

Blob scaled across 5 cities during COVID and fulfilled 1,000+ orders. A delivery-partner pilot improved fulfillment reliability and showed that logistics is the key driver of a scalable marketplace.
